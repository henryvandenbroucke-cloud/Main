# STORY: "What If You Could See Everyone's Future Job?"

Step 1 output (2026-11-12). This is a beat plan, not the voiceover. The "line ideas" only show what each beat must say. Step 2 writes the real script. Facts are referenced by ID (F1-F9) from `FACTS.md`.

Mode: second person, "Imagine you're...", present tense. Target 60-65 s, about 237 words at 3.8 words/s.

## Hook (sentence 1, no setup)
**"You can see everyone's future job, and your best friend's is: your boss."** (13 words)

How I tested it:
| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| 10-16 words, no setup | Pass: 13 words. |
| A surprise stated as fact | Pass: the goofy best friend outranks you. The ending reverses it. |
| Can you SEE it in frame 1? | Pass: the icon over Kevin's head shows tiny Kevin in a necktie and tiny you handing him coffee. |
| Does the ending pay it off? | Pass: the last line, "Now you're his boss," loops straight back into the hook. |
| Risk | "best friend's is: your boss" needs a short beat before "your boss." Check the TTS read in step 2. |

Spare hooks if the TTS read is clumsy:
- B: "You can see everyone's future job, and your best friend will be your boss." (14 words)
- C: "Over every head floats their future job, and your best friend's is your boss." (14 words)

## The power, in one line
**Over every head floats a tiny picture of the job that person will have in ten years, but it shows where they're headed today, so every choice can flip it.**

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| What you see | A small round icon (a picture, never words) floating just above each head. |
| How far ahead | 10 years. |
| How accurate | It forecasts each person's current path, not their fate. When a person makes a choice that changes their direction, the icon flips. |
| Who sees it | Only you. Nobody else notices the icons. |
| Your own icon | You can't see above your own head. It only shows in a reflection. |
| Jobs that don't exist yet | The icon shows an object you don't recognise. |

Continuity rules that follow from this:
1. The hero has **no** icon in any normal shot (the camera sees what he sees). His icon appears only in the mirror (beat 8).
2. No mirror, dark window or shiny steel may show the hero's reflection before beat 8. Watch the burger counter's steel and the street's glass.

## Icon design (NO TEXT LABELS: project rule)
- **Badge:** a round cream disc with an ink outline and a soft warm glow. About 40% of a head wide, floating about a third of a head above the head. It bobs gently (about 1.5 s per cycle) and always faces the camera (a billboard card, like the characters).
- **Pop-in:** scale 0 > 1.15 > 1 over about 6 frames, plus a 4-frame sparkle sprite.
- **Flip:** a Y-axis flip over about 8 frames. The picture swaps on the edge-on frame, with a small sparkle burst.
- **Inside:** ONE prop, readable by its shape first, 2-3 flat colours, ink outline. A two-figure mini scene only where the joke needs "you" (Kevin).
- **Never inside an icon:** letters, numbers, logos, flags, dollar signs, question marks or clock digits.

| Person | Icon | Must read as |
|---|---|---|
| Dad | Open-end wrench (the same as the real one by his plate) | "Same as now" |

| Kevin (start) | Tiny Kevin in a necktie behind a big desk, tiny you holding out a coffee cup | "Your boss" |

## Beats
Budget = planned words in the voiceover. Times are at 3.8 words/s. Escalation: harmless > funny > uncomfortable > personal.

### 1. HOOK: flash-forward (0.0-3.4 s, 13 words)
- **SEE:** school hallway, day. Kevin at his locker, mid-bite of a sandwich. His icon POPS: tiny Kevin in a tie at a big desk, tiny you handing him coffee. Cut to you: jaw drops, your juice box squirts (impact kick). A white 4-frame flash ends the hook.
- **Line:** the hook above.

### 2. SETTING and RULES (3.4-9.2 s, 22 words)
- **SEE:** family dinner, warm lamp, wide shot. Pop, pop, pop: icons appear over Dad, Mom and sister on three beats of the line. Your fork freezes halfway to your mouth. Dad's icon is the same wrench lying by his plate (a silent gag: the voiceover does not mention it).
- **Line idea:** "Imagine you're seventeen, and halfway through family dinner, a tiny picture pops up over every head: their job in ten years."
- **Rule shown:** the 10-year window. (The "it can change" rule is held back for the twist.)

### 3. FAMILY DINNER: harmless (9.2-16.0 s, 26 words) [F5]
- **SEE:** your sister in a cardboard helmet waves a toy rocket. Her icon is a phone on a ring light. Your eyes go from the helmet to the ring light. For the fact, a quick daydream cutaway: a row of kid cards with icons over them, mostly ring lights and only a few helmets (for example 8 to 3, which matches 29% to 11%).
- **Doubter:** sister: "I'm going to the moon!"
- **Line idea:** in one survey, American and British kids picked YouTuber over astronaut nearly three to one.

### 4. BEST FRIEND: funny (16.0-22.3 s, 24 words)
- **SEE:** next day, school hallway. Kevin tugs at a locker that isn't his. The hook's icon is over his head; we see it again from a new angle and hold on it.
- **Doubter:** Kevin: "Me? I can't even open my locker."
- **Line idea:** Kevin, who once ate a glue stick on a dare, is going to be your boss.

### 5. THE BULLY: funny, then uncomfortable (22.3-27.0 s, 18 words) [F7 optional]
- **SEE:** a locker slams shut beside your head (impact kick, dutch tilt). The bully looms. His icon is a sparkling tooth and a drill. Then a 1-2 s daydream: you in a dentist's chair, looking up at the bully leaning in with a whirring cartoon drill, in cooler light. Keep it comic: no injury, no needles.
- **Doubter:** bully: "What are you looking at?"
- **Line idea:** he's going to be a dentist. Your dentist. (Optional: "...and make about a hundred seventy-six thousand dollars a year doing it.")

### 6. THE STRANGER: weird, and it plants the mystery (27.0-31.2 s, 16 words) [F3/F4 optional]
- **SEE:** street, late afternoon, on your way to work. A woman at the bus stop has an icon you can't place: the strange glass bead. You tilt your head like a confused dog and squint. Its light blinks back at you.
- **Line idea:** a stranger's icon is something you've never seen: a job that doesn't exist yet.
- **Optional fact (only if there is room):** by 2018, about six in ten American workers had job titles that didn't exist in 1940 (F3). Never the 65% myth (F10).

### 7. THE BOSS: uncomfortable (31.2-38.6 s, 28 words) [F6]
- **SEE:** burger place, golden light. The manager hands you a paper hat. His icon is the very same paper hat. Behind him hangs a wall of employee-of-the-month frames: every photo is him, getting a little balder each time (blank plaques, no text). You look down at the paper hat in your hands. The camera creeps in. A fryer bubbles in the silence.
- **Doubter:** manager: "Stick with me, kid. One day, all this could be yours."
- **Line idea:** the typical restaurant worker has been at their job about two years. He's been here nineteen.

### 8. "WAIT...?" and the MIRROR TWIST (38.6-45.4 s, 26 words)
- **SEE:** night, your bathroom. You lean into the mirror. In the reflection, over your head: a paper hat (the manager's!). You grab your textbook (its cover shows a heart picture): the icon FLIPS to a stethoscope. You grab your guitar: microphone. You grab your phone: paper hat again. The flips speed up with each grab, ending on one flip to the stranger's unknown bead.
- **Line idea:** "Wait... if everyone has one, what's over your head?" Then: yours changes every time you make a choice.
- **Note:** let the flips carry the montage. The voiceover gives the meaning, not a list of what we already see.

### 9. "YOU SEE," PIVOT: the real mechanism (45.4-53.8 s, 32 words) [F1, F2 optional]
- **SEE:** night-blue. The icon in the mirror melts into a map pin on a little cartoon road. The road bends, and the pin swings to a new spot (no street names, no arrival-time digits). Then: a cartoon man walks a long road from 18 to grey-haired 58. About 13 icons pop and flip over his head along the way, packed closer together in the first stretch (F2). You can count them; no numbers on screen.
- **Line idea:** "You see," the icon isn't a prophecy. It's like your GPS arrival time: it shows where you'll end up if nothing changes, and it recalculates every turn. And people turn a lot: Americans born around 1960 held almost thirteen jobs by fifty-eight.
- **Trap:** "jobs" (employers), never "careers" (F1).

### 10. DEADPAN DOUBLE LINE (53.8-57.0 s, 12 words)
- **SEE:** in the mirror you grin, relieved ("Luckily"). Then the grin freezes and slides off your face ("Unluckily"). On the last word, cut to the next morning.
- **Line idea:** "Luckily, that means your future can change. Unluckily, so can everyone else's."
- Spare: "Luckily, nothing's set in stone. Unluckily, that includes Kevin."

### 11. IRONIC PAYOFF (57.0-62.3 s, 20 words)
- **SEE:** next morning, the hallway, framed like beat 1. You tell Kevin. He grins, tosses his homework over his shoulder into the bin, puts his feet up and shuts his eyes. His icon FLIPS: now tiny you wears the necktie and tiny Kevin holds the coffee. The flip lands on "boss." That is the last frame, and it loops straight into the hook.
- **Line idea:** so you tell Kevin he'll be your boss. He's so sure, he stops trying. His icon flips. Now you're his boss.
- **Why it's ironic:** the hero tried to read the future, and saying it out loud is the choice that changed it.

## No-text and readability checklist (for steps 3-4)
- Manager's name tag: blank. Employee-of-the-month frames: photos only, blank plaques.
- Textbook cover: a heart picture, no title. Phone screens: a picture, no interface text.
- No EXIT signs, no posters with words, no locker numbers (use colour dots), no bus route numbers. The burger sign is a big burger shape. The bus stop sign is a bus pictogram.
- Bully's jacket: a lightning-bolt patch, not a letter.
- GPS cutaway: road and pin only, no street names or arrival-time digits. No clocks with digits.
- No flags.
- Kevin's two-figure icon is the hardest read. Test it in 0.5-scale stills: tiny you must be recognisable by the orange hoodie.
- The hero's reflection must never appear before beat 8 (see the continuity rules).
- The dentist daydream and the locker slam stay cartoon-comic: no injury, no needles, no blood.

- Optional final touch in beat 11: a trophy-case glass behind the two boys reflects the hero with a necktie icon. Only if it reads clearly.
