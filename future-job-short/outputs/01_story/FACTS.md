## F1. People hold about 13 jobs by age 58: CORE
- **Claim:** Americans born from 1957 to 1964 held an average of 12.9 jobs from ages 18 to 58.
- **Source quote:** "Individuals born from 1957 to 1964 held an average of 12.9 jobs from ages 18 to 58."
- **Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, *Number of Jobs, Labor Market Experience, Marital Status, and Health* (National Longitudinal Survey of Youth 1979), released 26 Aug 2025, reissued 21 Jul 2026 to fix table titles. https://www.bls.gov/news.release/nlsoy.nr0.htm
- **Confidence:** HIGH.
- **Safe wording:** "Americans born around 1960 held almost thirteen jobs by age fifty-eight."
- **Traps:**
  - BLS counts a job as "an uninterrupted period of work with a particular employer" (technical note: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/nlsoy.tn.htm). It counts employers, not careers. **Never say "13 careers" or "13 professions."**
  - It is one US group (born 1957-1964). Do not say "everyone" or "the average person in the world."
  - The older figure, 12.7 jobs from ages 18 to 56 (2023 release, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/nlsoy_08222023.htm), is out of date. Use 12.9.

## F3. Most of today's jobs are "new": optional, the honest version of the 65% myth
- **Claim:** By 2018, roughly 60% of US employment was in job titles that did not exist in 1940.
- **Source quote (published version):** "the majority of contemporary work as of 2018—roughly 60%—is found in new job titles added since 1940." The working paper says: "Roughly 60% of employment in 2018 is found in job titles that did not exist in 1940."
- **Sources:**
  - Autor, Chin, Salomons and Seegmiller, "New Frontiers: The Origins and Content of New Work, 1940-2018," *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 139(3), 2024, pp. 1399-1465. PDF: https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2026-04/New-Frontiers-QJE-v139%283%29-p1399-1465-Aug-2024.pdf
  - NBER Working Paper 30389 (2022): https://www.nber.org/papers/w30389
- **Confidence:** HIGH (I read the sentence in both PDFs).
- **Method in one line:** the authors counted new job titles that Census coders added to the Census Alphabetical Index of Occupations each decade.
- **Safe wording:** "By 2018, about six in ten American workers had job titles that didn't exist in 1940."
- **Traps:** this is about US job titles, looking back over 78 years. It is NOT a forecast and NOT about children. Do not turn it into "60% of future jobs don't exist yet." The authors also say most new work grows out of old work (for example, a solar electrician is an electrician with a new skill).

## F5. Kids pick YouTuber over astronaut: CORE (the family-dinner joke)
- **Claim:** In a 2019 Harris Poll survey for LEGO, kids aged 8-12 in the US and UK picked YouTuber/vlogger as their top dream job (29%) and astronaut as their last (11%). In China it was the other way round: astronaut came first (56%).
- **Source quote (LEGO press release):** "today's children are three times more likely to aspire to be a YouTuber (29%) than an Astronaut (11%)". Survey "conducted online by The Harris Poll on behalf of LEGO among kids from China (n=1,000), the United States (n=1,000), and the United Kingdom (n=1,000) aged 8-12 (May 30 to June 8, 2019)."
- **Sources:**
  - LEGO Group press release, July 2019: https://www.lego.com/ms-my/aboutus/news/2019/july/lego-group-kicks-off-global-program-to-inspire-the-next-generation-of-space-explorers-as-nasa-celebrates-50-years-of-moon-landing
  - Country ranking (US/UK: YouTuber first, astronaut fifth; China: astronaut first): CNBC, 19 Jul 2019: https://www.cnbc.com/2019/07/19/more-children-dream-of-being-youtubers-than-astronauts-lego-says.html
- **Confidence:** HIGH for 29% vs 11% (press release). MEDIUM that these two numbers are the US-and-UK figures (the release says "today's children"; CNBC's country table shows they are).
- **Safe wording:** "In one survey, American and British kids picked YouTuber over astronaut nearly three to one." (29 / 11 = 2.6, so "nearly three to one" is fair. Do not say "three times.")
- **Traps:** do not say "kids everywhere" (China was the reverse). It is what kids *want*, not what they become.

## F6. Restaurant workers rarely stay long: CORE (the joke behind the manager beat)
- **Claim:** In January 2024, the median wage and salary worker in "food services and drinking places" had been with their current employer for 2.0 years. All workers: 3.9 years. Leisure and hospitality: 2.1 years.
- **Sources:**
  - BLS, *Employee Tenure in 2024* (26 Sep 2024), Table 5 (by industry): https://www.bls.gov/news.release/tenure.t05.htm
  - Overall median of 3.9 years: https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/median-tenure-with-current-employer-was-3-9-years-in-january-2024.htm
- **Confidence:** HIGH.
- **Safe wording:** "The typical restaurant worker has been at their job about two years. He's been here nineteen." (The 19 years is fiction: our manager is invented.)
- **Traps:** median tenure measures how long people have been at their job *so far*. It is not how long a job lasts. Do not say "restaurant workers quit after two years."

## F10. DEBUNKED: "65% of kids will have jobs that don't exist yet". NEVER USE
- **The claim:** "65% of children entering primary school today will end up working in jobs that don't yet exist."
- **Status:** nobody has found a study behind it.
  - Chalkbeat (21 Nov 2017) calls it a "made-up statistic." It says a BBC report traced its popularity to Cathy Davidson's 2011 book, and that Davidson "no longer uses the figure": https://www.chalkbeat.org/2017/11/21/21103817/to-back-up-claim-that-schools-must-change-devos-cites-made-up-statistic-about-the-future-of-work/
  - BBC World Service *More or Less*, "Have 65% of future jobs not yet been invented?" (May 2017). The episode could not find a source, and its job-title comparison suggested about a third at most. Programme page: https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p053ln9f (could not load it from this server; the details are from Davidson's reply and secondary reports).
  - Cathy Davidson's own reply (31 May 2017): https://teaching-learning.hastac.hcommons.org/2017/05/31/65-of-future-jobs-havent-been-invented-yet-cathy-davidson-responds-to-cathy-davidson-and-the-bbc. Search summaries of it say she took the number from an earlier futurist book (Jim Carroll, 2007) and could not reach the original authors. The page blocked this server (HTTP 403), so I could not read it myself.
  - Background on how the number spread (Shift Happens videos, the World Economic Forum's 2016 report): https://edtechbooks.org/wild/jobs_that_dont_exist
- **Confidence that it is debunked:** HIGH (no source was ever found). The BBC's "about a third" is MEDIUM (secondary reports only), so do not quote it either.
- **Also avoid:** "85% of the jobs of 2030 haven't been invented yet" (an Institute for the Future / Dell forecast from 2017, which is opinion, not data).
- **Use instead:** F3 (about 60% of 2018 jobs had titles that didn't exist in 1940). It is backward-looking and sourced.
