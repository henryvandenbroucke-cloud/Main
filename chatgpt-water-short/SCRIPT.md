## Facts (each line in the script uses only these)
| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Sam Altman, June 2025: an average ChatGPT query "uses about 0.000085 gallons of water; roughly one fifteenth of a teaspoon" (about 0.32 ml). | [The Gentle Singularity](https://blog.samaltman.com/the-gentle-singularity) (read 14 Nov 2026) |
| F2 | UC Riverside and UT Arlington researchers (Li, Yang, Islam, Ren): GPT-3 "needs to drink a 500ml bottle of water for roughly 10-50 medium-length responses, depending on when and where it is deployed". That is 10-50 ml per answer. | [arXiv 2304.03271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03271) (the sentence is in the paper body, quoted by search results; the abstract I read gives 700,000 litres to train GPT-3) |
| F3 | The gap: 50 ml / 0.32 ml = about 155 times; 10 ml / 0.32 ml = about 31 times. Script says "up to a hundred and fifty times". | F1 and F2 |
| F4 | Why the numbers differ: Altman's figure covers on-site cooling water only; the researchers also count the water used to generate the electricity. | [arXiv review 2601.06174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06174), [waterfreechat.com summary](https://waterfreechat.com/blog/chatgpt-water-usage-per-query) |
| F5 | Most large data centers cool by evaporation: water absorbs server heat and most of it leaves as vapour. Many use drinking-quality water. | [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/01/04/how_datacenters_use_water/), [HowStuffWorks](https://electronics.howstuffworks.com/everyday-tech/why-do-data-centers-need-water.htm) |
| F6 | A large data center can use 1-5 million gallons of water a day, as much as a town of 10,000-50,000 people (Washington Post, 2023, as quoted in search results). | Search summary of [HowStuffWorks](https://electronics.howstuffworks.com/everyday-tech/why-do-data-centers-need-water.htm); second-hand, not read at the source |
| F7 | ChatGPT users send about 2.5 billion prompts a day (OpenAI, July 2025). | [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/21/chatgpt-users-send-2-5-billion-prompts-a-day/) |
| F8 | Altman, 16 April 2025: people saying "please" and "thank you" costs "tens of millions of dollars well spent — you never know". He was talking about electricity cost, not water. | [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/310105/20250423/chatgpt-ceo-gladly-loses-tens-millions-when-ai-users-are-polite-money-well-spent.htm) |
| F9 | Beef: 15,415 litres of water per kg (global average, Water Footprint Network, Mekonnen and Hoekstra). A quarter-pound patty (113 g) is about 1,750 litres, about 460 gallons. Most of that is rain on pasture and feed crops. | From memory, not re-checked online today. Check before publishing. |
| F10 | 1,750 litres / 50 ml = about 35,000 answers per burger, using the highest figure in F2. | F2 and F9 |

Wording rules that keep it honest:
- "takes a sip" is a picture, not a measurement. The real per-question amount is between a fifteenth of a teaspoon and a few tablespoons.
- Say "an older version" for F2 (it was GPT-3, in 2023).
- F9 says "counting everything the cow eats and drinks", because most of that water is rain.
- The hot-dog question and "it might remember who didn't" are jokes, not facts.

## Why this should get comments
- It ends on a question that splits people in two (the polite and the not polite), and the last line gives both sides a reason to answer.
- The hot dog and sandwich line is a second argument starter in the first 10 seconds.
- The last picture (the robot sipping) is the first picture, so the video loops.
