## Facts (sourced)
- **Pin boys:**
  - Before automatic machines, boys and young men were hired to reset pins by hand and return the balls.
  - Teenagers sat on ledges above the lanes, cleared fallen pins, reset them and returned the balls.
  - The work was fast and dangerous, and pinboys were constantly injured.
  - Pay was about a nickel a game.
  - Sources: [Pinsetter (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinsetter), [International Bowling Museum](https://www.bowlingheritage.com/item/a-pinspotter-pioneer/), [History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/bowling-history-egypt-to-america).
- **The machine:**
  - The first mechanical pinsetter was invented by Gottfried Schmidt, who sold the patent to AMF in 1941.
  - AMF showed its Pinspotter in 1946, and it went into full production and installation in 1952.
  - Brunswick followed in the mid-1950s. Automatic pinsetters drove bowling's 1950s boom and ended the pin-boy job.
  - Sources: [Smithsonian Lemelson Center](https://invention.si.edu/invention-stories/set-em-knock-em-down-bowlings-automated-pin-technology), [Bunk History](https://bunkhistory.org/resources/bowling-for-suburbia).
