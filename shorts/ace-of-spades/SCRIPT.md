## Facts (sourced)
- From 1765 the Stamp Office printed the Ace of Spades itself, carrying the maker's name, as proof the duty on the pack had been paid. Card makers printed the other 51 cards and bought the duty ace from the Stamp Office. Forging it was a capital offence.
  - Sources: [World of Playing Cards: The Ace of Spades](https://wopc.co.uk/blogs/ken-lodge/72-the-ace-of-spades), [Fakes, forgeries and tax evasion](https://wopc.co.uk/blogs/ken-lodge/73-fakes-forgeries-and-tax-evasion).
- Richard Harding, a licensed London card maker, was tried at the Old Bailey in September 1805 for forging the stamp. A printer from the Stamp Office (Mr. Hockley) had bought packs from Harding and his apprentice containing counterfeit aces. Harding was hanged on 13 November 1805.
  - Source: [Richard Harding (forger) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Harding_(forger)).
- The duty on playing cards ended in 1960.
  - Source: [Richard Harding (forger) (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Harding_(forger)).

## Hook research (skill style bible + our retention data)
- Sentence 1 is a paradox stated as fact, about 10-16 words, with no setup. The voice starts by frame 4.
- Line 2 raises the stakes.
- Our brick-tax short loses viewers in the first seconds and then plateaus around 75%, so the first 3 seconds carry maximum motion.
