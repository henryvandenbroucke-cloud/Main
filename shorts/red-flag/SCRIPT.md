# The Man Who Walked in Front of Cars (Britain's "Red Flag" law)

## Facts (sourced)
- **Locomotive Act 1865:**
  - speed limits of 4 mph in the country and 2 mph in towns;
  - a crew of three for road locomotives, one of whom walked at least 60 yards ahead carrying a red flag.
  - Sources: [Locomotive Acts (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive_Acts), [Red flag traffic laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_flag_traffic_laws).
- **Highways and Locomotives (Amendment) Act 1878:**
  - dropped the red flag requirement;
  - the man on foot stayed, at least 20 yards ahead, to help with horses;
  - the speed limits stayed.
- **Locomotives on Highways Act 1896:**
  - light locomotives under 3 tons were freed from the earlier Acts;
  - the limit was raised to 14 mph (cut to 12 mph by regulation before it took effect).
- **Emancipation Run, 14 November 1896, London to Brighton:**
  - Lord Winchilsea ceremonially tore up a red flag at the start breakfast.
  - Sources: [VCC history](https://vccofgb.co.uk/london-to-brighton-veteran-car-run-history/), [Motor Sport archive](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/november-1990/57/brighton-run/).
- **The London to Brighton Veteran Car Run** commemorates it. It has been held most years since 1927, on the first Sunday in November.
