# The Robbers Who Never Broke a Lock (the Brink's job, Boston 1950)

## Why this story
- It follows the viral-twist formula of the reference Shorts:
  1. an impossible first line ("didn't break a single lock");
  2. a clever routine repeated ("door after door, key after key");
  3. the perfect crime, and nobody can touch them;
  4. a ticking clock (six years);
  5. "Then one of them…";
  6. a punchline that calls back to the hook ("a key for every door… couldn't keep one mouth shut").
- It's simple words, one storyline and no dates to memorise, so anyone can follow it.
- **Competition:** the top existing Brink's Short had 1.4K views.

## Facts and sources
- **The robbery:** 17 January 1950, about 7:10 pm, at the Brink's building in Boston's North End.
  - The take was $2,775,395: $1,218,211 in cash plus $1,557,184 in cheques, money orders and securities.
  - It was then the largest robbery in US history.
- **The keys:** over several months the crew removed the lock cylinders from the doors, had keys copied, and put the locks back.
- **The gang:** 11 men in all. Seven went inside, in pea coats, chauffeur's caps and rubber Halloween masks, with rubber soles to muffle their steps. Two waited in the getaway car, and one lookout signalled with a flashlight from a rooftop.
- **The night:** five aborted attempts came before the go-ahead. They were inside about 20 minutes, with no shots fired and no locks forced.
  - The employees were tied up, and about $1 million in coins was left behind because it was too heavy to carry.
  - The police found only a cap, plus the rope and tape.
- **The rule:** the gang agreed not to touch the money for six years, the Massachusetts statute of limitations.
- **Specs O'Keefe:**
  - He was arrested for another burglary in 1950.
  - His share, given to a gang member for safekeeping, was never seen again.
  - He kidnapped another member to get it, and the gang hired a hitman ("Trigger" Burke), who shot him but didn't kill him.
  - The FBI approached him, and in January 1956 he agreed to talk.
- **The arrests:** they came on 12 January 1956, about five days before the six-year deadline.
  - Eight men got life sentences.
  - Only about $58,000 was ever recovered.

## Look (studied from @segmentzero, whose top Shorts reach 1.7–5.2M views)
- **Close shots:** characters fill the frame in medium close-ups (head and chest, at 1.6–2.4× zoom), with fewer wide shots.
- **Expressive faces:** gritted-teeth grimaces, big emotional eyes, sweat drops and brow pops. Every character has one signature item:
  - Specs has round glasses;
  - the gang wear rubber masks and chauffeur caps;
  - the FBI wear fedoras;
  - the locksmith has a jeweller's loupe.
- **Depth of field:** strong blur on the 3D background behind the sharp cast, with warm key light and light shafts.
- **Captions:** smaller, cleaner single words.
- **Motion:** something moves every beat.
