## Facts and sources
- **Who he was:** Joseph Hobson Jagger (1830–1892), a Yorkshire textile and mill man from Bradford. He reasoned that, like spinning machinery, roulette wheels were never perfectly balanced.
- **The clerks:** he hired six clerks to record the results at the six wheels of the Casino de Monte-Carlo. On one wheel, nine numbers came up far more often than chance (popularly given as 7, 8, 9, 17, 18, 19, 22, 28 and 29).
- **The winnings:** he bet on that wheel and, by repute, won over 2 million francs over several days. The figures vary, so the script says "the story goes".
- **The swap:** the casino moved the wheels between tables. Jagger is said to have recognised his wheel by a small scratch or mark.
- **The fix:** the casino then fitted movable partitions, or frets, so no wheel stayed biased.
- **After:** he stopped, went home and bought houses in Bradford.

## Shot plan
1. **Hook:** frame 1 is an extreme close-up of a spinning roulette wheel with the ball rattling. The camera snaps back over the gilded salon, and Jagger (top hat, mutton chops) watches with his arms folded. A coin stays in his pocket.
2. **The clerks:** six clerks with notebooks sit at the wheels, and the tally marks fill up fast. "Every day" shows a calendar flipping.
3. **The mill flashback:** a Yorkshire mill with a lopsided flywheel wobbling. Then the roulette wheel wobbles the same way.
4. **"Five random… the sixth":** five notebooks scatter evenly. The sixth has nine numbers circled red, and the numbers glow on the wheel.
5. **The betting:** chips slide onto the nine numbers, the ball drops in, and the stacks grow, again and again.
6. **"Two million francs":** the counter rolls, the croupier sweats, and a black cloth is laid over the broken table.
7. **The swap:** night, darkness, porters secretly rolling the wheels to different tables.
8. **The scratch:** Jagger loses (his face falls), then a magnifier finds a tiny scratch on the rim. He points to the other table and wins again.
9. **The fix:** a workman fits new movable dividers into every wheel with a screwdriver, and the director is relieved.
10. **The end:** golden-hour Yorkshire street, Jagger with the keys to a row of houses.
11. **Punchline:** the notebook closes. "He beat it with a notebook."
