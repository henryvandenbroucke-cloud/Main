[mysterious] A man broke the bank at Monte Carlo... [short pause] [mischievously] and for the first month, he didn't bet a single coin.

[curious] Instead, he hired six clerks. Each one sat at a roulette wheel... and wrote down every number. All day. Every day.

[serious] Because Joseph Jagger was a mill engineer from Yorkshire. And he knew something about machines. Nothing spins perfectly. [short pause] Not a mill wheel... and not a roulette wheel.

[excited] He was right. Five wheels were perfectly random. But on the sixth... nine numbers kept coming up way too often.

[mischievously] So he started betting. Only on that wheel. Only on those nine numbers.

[impressed] Day after day, he kept winning. The story goes, he walked away with over two million francs.

[dramatic] The casino panicked. So one night, they secretly swapped the wheels between the tables.

[sighs] The next day, Jagger started losing... [short pause] [surprised] until he noticed something. His lucky wheel had a tiny scratch. He found it at another table... and kept on winning.

[deadpan] In the end, the casino rebuilt every wheel, so none of them could stay lopsided ever again.

[warm] And Jagger? He quit while he was ahead, went home to Yorkshire... and bought a row of houses.

[chuckles] He didn't beat the casino with luck. He beat it with a notebook.
