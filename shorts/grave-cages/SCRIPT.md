# "Why People Locked Their Dead in Cages" — original script v1 (2026-11-06)
Related to the reference channel's grave-bell short (graveyard history with a hidden reason), but our own topic,
story and characters: the mortsafe, built against the body snatchers who supplied anatomy schools.

## Facts (sources)
- Mortsafes: heavy iron cages / plates padlocked over a grave so resurrectionists could not reach the coffin.
  Invented early 19th century. Left about six weeks until the body was too decayed for dissection, then dug up and
  reused; churches bought them and hired them out, and mortsafe societies charged fees. (Wikipedia: Mortsafe)
- Anatomy schools needed fresh bodies; the only legal supply was executed criminals (Murder Act 1751). (Wikipedia: Body snatching)
- Snatchers dug at the head end, broke the lid, pulled the body out with a rope or hook, and put the clothes and
  belongings back: taking property was theft, but a body was not legally property. (Wikipedia: Body snatching, Mortsafe)
- Families and hired men watched new graves at night; watch-houses and watchtowers were built (Dalkeith tower 1827). (Wikipedia: Mortsafe)
- Burke and Hare murdered at least 16 people in Edinburgh and sold the bodies for dissection; Burke was hanged in 1829. (Wikipedia: Body snatching)
- The Anatomy Act 1832 gave doctors a legal supply of bodies; mortsafes fell out of use and most were broken up for
  iron. Some survive, e.g. Greyfriars Kirkyard, Edinburgh. (Wikipedia: Mortsafe)
- Online since ~2012, photos of grave cages are captioned as "to keep vampires in". (Snopes)

## Voice script (ElevenLabs eleven_v4, voice "Aaron Gerard")
[excited] In the 1800s, people in Scotland locked their dead relatives in iron cages. [mischievously] And no... it wasn't to stop vampires getting out.
[dramatically] It's Edinburgh, 1820s. A man is buried on Monday. By Wednesday... his grave is empty.
[curious] But his clothes? Still in the coffin. Nobody took his watch. Nobody took his shoes. [surprised] Somebody took HIM.
[whispers] You see, doctors needed bodies to learn surgery. [excited] And the law only gave them executed criminals. Nowhere near enough.
So at night, the resurrection men crept in. They dug at the head end, hooked the body with a rope, and dragged it out.
[mischievously] And they ALWAYS left the clothes behind. Because stealing clothes was theft... but a body didn't legally belong to anyone.
[angry] So families fought back. They sat by the grave all night with lanterns. They built watchtowers.
[dramatically] And then someone invented the mortsafe. A giant iron cage, padlocked right over the coffin.
[curious] Wait... how long do you keep your grandma in a cage? [short pause] About six weeks. Just long enough to be useless to a surgeon.
[laughs] Then the church dug the cage back up... and rented it to the next family.
[nervous] Then two men in Edinburgh, Burke and Hare, skipped the digging completely. They murdered sixteen people and sold the bodies.
[dramatically] The whole country panicked, and in 1832 a new law finally gave doctors a legal supply.
[sighs] The cages weren't needed anymore. Most were melted down for iron.
[warmly] But a few are still standing in Scottish graveyards today... [mischievously] where the internet swears they were built to stop vampires.
