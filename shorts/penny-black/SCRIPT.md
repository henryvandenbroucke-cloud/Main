## Hook
- **Paradox in the first sentence:** she *refused* the letter, and that is *how she read it*.
- **Frame 1:** tight on the letter being pushed back into the postman's hand. The camera snaps out to reveal his baffled face and her innocent smile, with a paper flick and a soft whoosh.
- The "it's blank" reveal is a mid-video payoff (about 18 s) to hold the plateau.
- The ending loops back to the opening ("saying no to the postman").

## Facts and sources
- **Recipient paid:** before 1840 postage was normally paid on delivery by the recipient, charged by distance and number of sheets. Recipients could refuse. [Victorian Web: The Penny Post](https://www.victorianweb.org/victorian/history/pennypos.html), [GBPS pre-stamp notes](https://gbps.org.uk/rates/inland/prestamp-notes.php)
- **Cost:** London–Edinburgh (396 miles) was charged 1s 1d per single sheet from 1812 to 1839 ([RPSL handout](https://rpsl.org.uk/rpsl/Displays/Handouts/DISP_20120607_001.pdf)). It could cost as much as a day's wages ([Victorian Web](https://www.victorianweb.org/victorian/history/pennypos.html)).
- **The Coleridge anecdote:**
  - As told by Harriet Martineau: in the Lake District, a cottage woman turned the letter over and returned it, saying she could not pay the postage, a shilling.
  - Coleridge paid it. Once the postman had gone, she showed that the sheet was blank: her brother sent one each quarter, so she had news of him free.
  - Sources: [Chest of Books, *Modern England* VI](https://www.chestofbooks.com/history/england/Modern-England/VI-Steam-Telegraph-And-Postage-Part-3.html), [Notes and Queries, 1851](https://litres.ru/book/various-10848091/notes-and-queries-number-62-january-4-1851-35494103/chitat-onlayn/?page=3).
  - The DNB disputes that this story *caused* Hill's reform, so the script never claims it did.
- **Code marks:** writers put a code mark on the outside of the letter. The addressee read it and refused delivery, avoiding the charge. [Victorian Web](https://www.victorianweb.org/victorian/history/pennypos.html)
- **Rowland Hill:** a schoolmaster (Hazelwood School). His 1837 pamphlet *Post Office Reform* proposed one penny per half-ounce anywhere, prepaid by the sender, with an adhesive label. [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/node/51450)
- **Dates:** Uniform Penny Post began on 10 January 1840. The Penny Black (Queen Victoria's head), the world's first adhesive postage stamp, went on sale on 1 May and was valid from 6 May 1840. [Postal Museum](https://staging1.postalmuseum.org/?p=4601), [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/penny-post-sparked-letter-writing-revolution/)
- **Letter volume:** 76 million letters in 1839, about 168–169 million in 1840. [Victorian Web](https://www.victorianweb.org/victorian/history/pennypos.html)
