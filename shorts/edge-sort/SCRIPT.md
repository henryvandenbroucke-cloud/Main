# "For Luck": the superstitious card trick that won £7.7 million (Crockfords, 2012)

**Virality score: 88/100.** Gambling loopholes are the strongest repeat format on the reference channel (RicksClips: 6M–15M per Short). Clips about this exact case reach 3.5M and 2.4M views, but none is an animated story. The twist is clean and court-documented.

## Facts and sources
- **The win:** over two days in August 2012, professional gambler Phil Ivey and Cheung Yin Sun played punto banco (baccarat) at Crockfords, London, and won about £7.7m.
- **The trick:** Ms Sun, claiming to be superstitious, asked the croupier to turn certain cards (the strong sevens, eights and nines) a different way round. Ivey asked to keep playing with the same cards.
  - The cards' back pattern was not perfectly symmetrical, so the turned cards could be recognised the next time they came out of the shoe. This technique is called **edge-sorting**.
- **The courts:** Crockfords refused to pay. The High Court (2014), the Court of Appeal (2016) and the **UK Supreme Court, unanimously, on 25 October 2017** held that it was cheating, in breach of his contract with the casino.
- **America:** the Borgata in Atlantic City sued over the same technique. Ivey was ordered to repay about **$10.1 million**, and the parties settled in 2020.
