## Facts (sourced)
- **The painting:** Goya's *Portrait of the Duke of Wellington* went on display at the National Gallery and was stolen 19 days later, on 21 August 1961. Kempton Bunton, a 61-year-old Newcastle pensioner, said he climbed in through a loosened toilet window.
  - Sources: [Kempton Bunton (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempton_Bunton), [National Archives blog](https://blog.nationalarchives.gov.uk/the-amazing-story-behind-the-duke/).
- **The investigation:** Police first assumed an expert art thief.
- **His motive:** Bunton objected to the TV licence fee and campaigned for free licences for pensioners. He had served time for refusing to pay. His ransom notes asked for money to fund TV licences for the elderly.
  - Source: [ITV](https://itv.com/news/2021-08-19/ransom-notes-released-60-years-after-portrait-theft-in-bid-for-free-tv-licences).
- **The film joke:** In the 1962 film *Dr. No*, the painting is displayed in the villain's lair.
  - Source: [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/art/the-duke-goya-art-heist-history).
- **The return:** The painting was returned in 1965. Six weeks later, on 19 July 1965, Bunton surrendered.
- **The trial:**
  - Defence counsel Jeremy Hutchinson argued it was only theft if he meant to keep it for good.
  - Verdict: Not Guilty of stealing the portrait; Guilty of stealing the frame, which was never returned. Three months in Wandsworth.
  - The government then changed the law to make removing items from public galleries an offence.
  - Sources: [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/goyas-wellington-duke-disappears), [Salon](https://www.salon.com/2015/11/14/the_wackiest_art_heist_ever_hardly_a_thomas_crown_affair_this_real_theft_of_a_masterpiece_was_a_little_bit_007_and_a_whole_lot_monty_python/).
- **The son:** A file released in 2012 showed Bunton's son John had confessed to the theft after a 1969 arrest.
  - Source: [Kempton Bunton (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempton_Bunton).
